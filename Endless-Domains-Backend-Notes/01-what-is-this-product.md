# 01. What This Product Actually Is

## Reading a codebase from the outside in

Every project you have notes on before this one was a teaching example, built to demonstrate one or two ideas cleanly. This one is the opposite, a real, live company's production backend, and the first skill worth practicing on it is one nobody teaches directly, figuring out what a system actually does by reading its code, its dependencies, and its module names, before anyone explains it to you. That is exactly what a fullstack engineer does on their first week at almost any job, so this note is deliberately built the way you would build your own understanding, from the outside evidence in, rather than from a description handed to you.

## The evidence

The `package.json` names the project `endless-domain-api`, and its dependency list is the fastest way to see the whole shape of the business at a glance. It has `ethers`, `web3`, `web3-utils`, `moralis`, `alchemy-sdk`, and `@moralisweb3/*`, which are all libraries for talking to blockchains. It has `argon2`, `bcrypt`, `passport`, `passport-jwt`, `speakeasy`, and `qrcode`, which are password hashing, authentication, and two factor authentication libraries. It has `stripe` style integrations (visible later as a whole feature folder), plus `@sendgrid/mail` for email, `@aws-sdk/client-s3` for file storage, `@aws-sdk/client-secrets-manager` for centrally managed configuration, and `@anthropic-ai/sdk`, meaning it talks to Claude for at least one real feature. The `contracts` folder at the project root holds two Solidity smart contract files, `EndlessCollection.sol` and `EndlessToken.sol`. The `src/components` folder, the heart of the app, has folders named `domain`, `marketplace`, `custom-domain`, `nft-collection`, `contract-deployment`, and one folder per blockchain the company supports, `ens-integration`, `bnb-integration`, `arb-integration`, `avax-integration`, `aptos-integration`, `starknet-integration`, `tezos-integration`, `ton-intigration`, plus integrations with two other domain registrars, `ud-integration` (Unstoppable Domains) and `freename`.

## What that adds up to

Endless Domains is a Web3 domain name marketplace. In plain terms, it lets a person register, buy, sell, and manage domain names that live on a blockchain rather than through a traditional registrar like GoDaddy, similar in concept to services like Ethereum Name Service (ENS) or Unstoppable Domains, except this company is trying to support many blockchains at once, rather than being tied to just one, which is exactly why there are so many separate, similarly shaped integration folders, one per chain the product supports. On top of that core product, it has an entire secondary layer of a normal SaaS business, user accounts, a marketplace for buying and reselling domains other people already own, an affiliate program, invoices, coupons, an admin panel, a blog, SEO tooling (Google Search Console and Google Analytics integrations), and, more recently, AI powered features for suggesting domain names, given the Anthropic dependency and a component folder literally called `ai-domain-discovery`.

## Why this matters before you read a single feature module

A frontend developer moving into fullstack work almost always meets a codebase this way, a business you do not fully understand yet, wrapped around code you are being asked to change safely. The habit worth building here, before opening `domain.service.ts` or any other single file, is asking what the module's name implies about the business, checking that against a real request or entity if you can find one, and only then trusting your own mental model of what it does. Every cluster of notes after this one already did that work for its slice of the app, but the skill is worth noticing being used, not just reading its output.

## Update from the October 2026 uat pull

The evidence in the code now points at one more direction for the business, and it is worth reading it the same way as above, from the code inward. A new folder, `src/components/marketplacev2`, and new secret keys named `SEAPORT_ADDRESS_POLYGON`, `USDT_ADDRESS_POLYGON` and `OPENSEA_API_KEY` say a lot before you open a single file. Seaport is the open marketplace protocol that OpenSea itself uses, USDT is a dollar stablecoin, and Polygon is a cheap, fast Ethereum compatible chain. Put together, the company is rebuilding its resale marketplace on the same standard as the largest NFT marketplace in the world, priced in a stable currency rather than a volatile token, on a chain where transactions cost cents. Because the orders follow Seaport's standard, tools and indexers that understand Seaport can in principle read them too, which is a strong reason for the change. The new `/market/stats`, `/market/series` and `/market/activity` endpoints pull sales, volume and listing figures for whole domain collections (ENS, Unstoppable Domains, SPACE ID, Freename) from OpenSea. That tells you the product now also wants to look like a market dashboard, showing activity across all Web3 domains, not only the ones sold on Endless Domains.
